# Korean personal gifting and celebration market: evidence for pricing a virtual "birthday billboard gift" (WISH BOARD paid tier)

Scope: Korea only. Today is 2026-11-22. All prices in KRW. "Confirmed" means the figure was read on the cited page (WebFetch). "Snippet" means it came from a search-result summary of the cited page and was not re-read in full, so verify before quoting. Older data is labelled with its year.

## 1. How big is KakaoTalk 선물하기 and the mobile-voucher (기프티콘) market?

### Takeaway
KakaoTalk 선물하기 is the default way Koreans send a small gift. In 2025 people sent about 189.5 million gifts on it, roughly 540,000 a day. Its voucher (교환권) sales alone ran at about 3.8조원 a year in 2023. The wider e-coupon market reached about 9.8조원 in 2023, and Kakao held about two-thirds of mobile gifting in 2022. Kakao does not publish a 선물하기-only annual total, an average gift price or a birthday share. The top items are low-priced vouchers (Starbucks, Baemin), and birthdays are the main occasion (74.5% in a 2023 survey).

### Inferences
- Rough estimate of the average gift price on 선물하기. Kakao's voucher sales alone were 3.8조원 in 2023 and were running at about 3.9조원 a year in early 2024. Dividing 3.8조 by 189.5 million gifts (2025 count) gives about 20,000원. Total 선물하기 sales are larger than voucher sales, so the true overall average is probably above 20,000원. This mixes years and assumes 2025 voucher sales are not lower than 2023, so treat it as a rough lower bound, not a measured figure.
- The most common gift is a coffee or delivery voucher. A "커피 한 잔 값" tier (about 4,000 to 6,000원) sits at the very bottom of normal Korean gifting. It reads as an add-on or a "small extra", not as the main gift. A 9,900원 or 14,900원 tier would still fall inside the everyday 1만 to 3만원 gifting band (see Section 3).
- Message cards are used by 75% of senders (2020) and birthdays are the top occasion (74.5%). Koreans already attach a personal message to a voucher. WISH BOARD's billboard is a richer version of the message card, so it could be positioned to sit next to a Kakao gift rather than replace it.
- Busy days are Pepero Day, Valentine's, Teacher's Day, White Day and the day before 수능. These are natural campaign dates beyond birthdays.

### Gaps
- No official 선물하기-only annual GMV for 2024 or 2025. Kakao reports only combined commerce GMV and growth rates.
- No published average order value, price-band distribution or birthday share for 선물하기. Kakao has never disclosed these in the sources found.
- No 2024 or 2025 full-year Statistics Korea e-coupon figure was retrieved, so 2023 (9.8조원) is the latest confirmed full year.
- No 2025 or 2026 KakaoTalk MAU figure was found in the Q3 2026 release.

## 2. What do Koreans pay for novelty and digital celebration products?

### Takeaway
Korean online rolling-paper apps found in this search are free. No public paid tier or price was found. Paid novelty celebration content is sold mostly as custom freelance work on 크몽, at about 40,000 to 180,000원 for celebration videos and 10,000 to 35,000원 for mobile wedding invitations. Hosting length is used as the upsell axis. Real fan birthday billboards in Seoul cost 1,000,000 to 7,000,000원 or more a month, and fans pool money for them. That anchors the "billboard" idea at the high end.

### Inferences
- Rolling paper is a free, commoditised category in Korea. A "group rolling-paper" feature probably cannot carry a price on its own. It works better as part of the paid tier, or as the hook that brings several payers to one gift.
- Custom celebration videos start at about 40,000 to 50,000원 and take days to make. An instant, automatic 15-second, high-quality personalised video at a few thousand won to about 10,000원 is a large discount against that anchor. The video is probably the most defensible paid element.
- Mobile invitations price by hosting period (+1 week, +1 month, +3 months). This supports making "permanent keepsake page" a premium attribute, or offering a time-limited free page that can be made permanent for an upgrade fee.
- Real subway birthday boards cost 1 to 7 million won a month and are funded by groups. A virtual Times Square billboard can be framed as "the fan-ad experience for the price of a coffee". Fandoms already pool money, so a group-funding option (e.g. 5 friends × 2,000원) matches existing behaviour. Times Square birthday ads are already a known fan ritual, so the setting itself has meaning.

### Gaps
- No Korean rolling-paper service with a public paid tier or price was found. Foreign services like Kudoboard were not researched because the scope is Korea only.
- No Korean KRW prices were found for star naming, personalised newspapers, birthday song products or digital greeting cards.
- 크몽 prices are seller listings from search snippets. Sales volumes and current prices were not verified page by page.
- The cost of a real Times Square fan ad was not found.

## 3. How do Koreans celebrate milestones, and what do they spend?

### Takeaway
Korean couples spend about 270,000 to 290,000원 on a partner's birthday gift (2023 survey). Gifts for smaller couple anniversaries mostly cost 10,000 to 50,000원 (2020 survey). Younger people are under clear cost pressure: in 2026, 71.2% of Gen Z said they cut friend-meeting spending, and about 50,000원 is the felt ceiling per get-together. No reliable survey on teen birthday-gift spending, or on 합격, 졸업 or 전역 spending, was found.

### Cited Findings
**Partner birthday gifts (가연 survey)**
- 가연 marriage-information company, n=1,000 unmarried adults (500 men, 500 women), published 2023-03-09. All confirmed. — [인사이트 2023-03-09](https://www.insight.co.kr/news/432030); [아시아경제 2023-03-08](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2023030815361061101)
  - Average spend per partner birthday: 280,300원, up 31,500원 from 248,800원 in December 2021.
  - By age: 20s 271,600원, 30s 284,800원.
  - By gender: women 293,000원, men 267,000원. In 2021 men spent more (257,000원 vs 240,600원).
  - Wanted gifts, women: cash 21.4%, electronics 19.7%, "heartfelt feelings" (마음) 19.4%.
  - Wanted gifts, men: "heartfelt feelings" 27.2%, electronics 23.8%, cash 19%.

**Couple anniversary gifts (듀오 survey, 2020, old)**
- 듀오, n=454 dating singles in their 20s and 30s, released 2020-05-13. All confirmed. — [인사이트 2020-05-20](https://www.insight.co.kr/news/284790)
  - Among couples who mark anniversaries: 30,000 to 50,000원 32.2%, 10,000 to 30,000원 31.3%, 50,000 to 70,000원 15.9%.
  - More than 60% spend 10,000 to 50,000원.
  - The survey does not name 100일 or 1주년 separately.

**Friendship cost pressure (2025 to 2026)**
- 알바천국, n=601 Gen Z and 544 millennials, fielded June 2026. All confirmed. — [다음/뉴스 2026-07-21](https://v.daum.net/v/20260721050223954)
  - 90.1% and 93% of the two groups found friend meetups financially burdensome.
  - 71.2% of Gen Z cut friend-meeting spending in the past year.
  - The felt ceiling per meetup is about 50,000원, and a typical meetup costs 30,000 to 50,000원.
  - Birthday and anniversary gifts were named as a cost driver by 25.8% of Gen Z.
- Trend Monitor "2025 소비생활 전망", n=1,000, ages 19 to 59. Spending people would cut first: dining out 40.4%, clothing 27.8%, social-gathering costs 26.7%. Confirmed. — [아시아경제 2025-08-21](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025082113512984519)
- Opinion column: gifticon amounts have inflated, from a 5,000원 iced americano to a two-person dessert set as the norm. Senders feel awkward giving less than they received. This is opinion, not data. Snippet. — [이투데이 기고](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2497974)

**Teens**
- 대학내일20대연구소 (2025), n=800 aged 14 to 18. Average monthly money (allowance plus earnings): boys about 230,000원, girls about 137,000원. Half have under 100,000원 a month. Snippet; the original report was not read. — [오픈애즈](https://www.openads.co.kr/content/contentDetail?contsId=20494)
- No survey was found on how much teens spend on friends' birthday gifts.

**Proposals**
- Korea Research "2025 결혼 인식 조사", n=1,000: 45% say a proposal is necessary, rising to 55% among ages 18 to 29. Snippet. — [아시아경제 2025-08-20](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025082013365286059)
- Five-star hotel proposal packages run from about 800,000원 to over 20,000,000원. Snippet; the article's year is unclear. — [다음 결혼 비용 명세서](https://v.daum.net/v/HMk5sug10N?f=p)
- A 2023 online debate asked whether a "프러포즈 200만원" is too much. Snippet. — [서울신문 2023-05-10](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/2023/05/10/20230510500086)

### Inferences
- Partner birthdays (about 280,000원) are high-budget occasions. A 5,000 to 15,000원 virtual billboard can be sold there as an add-on surprise ("on top of the real gift"), not as a replacement.
- Peer birthdays among students and young adults are budget-pressured and run on vouchers. A free tier plus a 3,000 to 5,000원 upgrade fits that, especially if several friends split the cost.
- Men list "heartfelt feelings" (마음) as their top wanted birthday gift (27.2%), and it is #3 for women. That supports positioning the billboard as an "emotional gift" rather than a novelty gadget.
- Proposals and 100-day or 1-year anniversaries are where people tolerate theatrical "big screen" displays (hotel packages, events). These suit a higher-priced spotlight tier.

### Gaps
- No 2023 to 2026 survey on spending for 100일 or 1주년 anniversaries, 합격, 졸업 or 전역 was found.
- No teen-specific birthday gift spending survey was found.
- The 듀오 anniversary data is from 2020. A newer equivalent was not found.

## 4. Willingness to pay for purely digital or virtual gifts, and price sensitivity

### Takeaway
Koreans already pay small amounts for purely digital goods. A KakaoTalk emoticon costs about 2,500원, and the Emoticon Plus subscription is 3,900원 a month on the web. The same subscription costs 6,900원 through iOS in-app payment, so the web price is notably cheaper. Emoticon Plus had about 2.5 million subscribers in 2024. Price sensitivity is high among young people, and discounts drive Toss gifting.

### Cited Findings
- Emoticon prices, reported June 2025. Snippet. — [IT동아 via 다음](https://v.daum.net/v/pdYlprhLvZ)
  - Single emoticon: 200 chocos is 2,500원 on both App Store and Google Play.
  - 300-choco emoticon: 3,800원 (App Store) or 3,750원 (Google Play).
  - Emoticon Plus subscription: 3,900원 a month on the web, 6,900원 via the iOS App Store, 5,700원 via Google Play. Web payment can be up to about half the iOS price.
- Kakao 13th anniversary (November 2024): Emoticon Plus reached 2.5 million subscribers, and 700,000 emoticons have been sent 280 billion times in total. Snippet. — [카카오 2024-11](https://www.kakaocorp.com/page/detail/11353)
- Kakao 14th anniversary (November 2025): 850,000 emoticons in total and more than 300 billion cumulative sends. Snippet. — [카카오 2025-11](https://www.kakaocorp.com/page/detail/11818?lang=ENG)

### Inferences
- 2,500원 (one emoticon set) and 3,900원 a month (unlimited emoticons) are the Korean reference prices for a purely digital, emotional item. A 4,900원 "coffee-price" billboard sits just above an emoticon. That is credible if the upgrade is clearly more personal: the person's name on a big screen, a video and a keepsake page.
- Kakao prices the same item at 3,900원 on the web and 6,900원 in the iOS app. That shows app-store fees are passed to users, and that Korean users accept "cheaper on the web". WISH BOARD is a web service and can keep web pricing and avoid the 15 to 30% app-store cut.
- Young Koreans pick gifting apps out of habit or for discounts. A small first-purchase discount, or a "free billboard + paid upgrade" structure, likely matters more than feature lists.

### Gaps
- No Korean survey was found measuring willingness to pay for virtual or digital gifts specifically, such as profile decorations, Zepeto items or digital cards.
- No 2025 or 2026 Emoticon Plus subscriber count was found.
- No data was found on gifting emoticons (이모티콘 선물하기) as a share of emoticon sales.

## 5. Payment habits and PG fees for a small web service

### Takeaway
Easy-pay services dominate online checkout in Korea. In 2025, 79% of online payers mainly used 간편결제. The three big players processed about 106조원, led by Naver Pay (53%). A new small web merchant should expect card and easy-pay fees of about 3.2 to 3.5% per transaction plus VAT, a sign-up fee of around 220,000원 and possibly an annual fee of about 110,000원. Preferential small-merchant rates apply only after revenue is reported.

### Cited Findings
**Easy-pay volume and share**
- Bank of Korea, H1 2025: easy-pay daily average of 1조464억원 (+11.4%), the first time above 1조원. Electronic financial companies (Naver Pay, KakaoPay, Payco, Toss Pay, etc.) had 55.1% of the value, and phone makers (Samsung Pay, Apple Pay) 23.9%. Snippet. — [한국경제 2025-09-29](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202509294322i)
- Bank of Korea, full-year 2025: daily average of 1조1,053억원 (+14.6%) and 35.57 million transactions a day (+14.9%). Snippet. — [트위그 2026-03-20](https://www.twig24.com/news/finance/2026/03/20/20260320500157); [아시아경제 2026-03-30](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026033011155780663)
- FSS data released by Rep. Yoo Dong-soo for 2025: Naver Pay, KakaoPay and Toss Pay together processed about 106조3,000억원 online and offline. Naver Pay processed about 56조5,000억원 (53%), KakaoPay about 37조8,000억원 and Toss 12조원. Snippet. — [전자신문 2026-05-08](https://www.etnews.com/20260508000205)
- Opensurvey 페이먼트 트렌드 2025, n=1,000 aged 20 to 64 who paid by mobile in the past month, published 2025-08-11. All confirmed. — [오픈서베이 2025](https://blog.opensurvey.co.kr/article/ds-payment-2025-2/)
  - 79.0% mainly used easy pay online, versus 53.7% for bank or card apps.
  - Main easy-pay service: Naver Pay 51.5%, KakaoPay 25.1%, Toss Pay 13.2%.
  - People in their 20s use easy pay relatively more.
- Opensurvey 2026: Toss is used for payments by 62.0% of teens. KakaoTalk is used for transfers and payments by 51.4% of people in their 20s. Confirmed. — [오픈서베이 2026](https://blog.opensurvey.co.kr/article/generation-z-alpha-2026-2/)

**PG fees for new or small online merchants (PortOne's 2026 comparison)**
- Typical rates. Confirmed. — [포트원 PG 비교 2026](https://blog.portone.io/opi_pg-comparison2026/)
  - Credit card: 3.2 to 3.5%, settled D+4 to D+5.
  - 간편결제 (easy pay): 3.2 to 3.5%.
  - Account transfer: 1.8 to 2%.
  - Virtual account: 300 to 400원 per transaction.
  - Mobile-phone billing: about 6 to 9%, settled D+30.
  - Direct PG sign-up fee: about 220,000원 on average. PortOne says its package waives this; that is its own claim.
  - Example small-merchant preferential rate: card fee 0.5% plus PG margin of about 1.3%, about 1.8% in total, plus VAT.
- PG company rates, from the same source. Confirmed.
  - Toss Payments: card fee 3.4%, annual management fee 110,000원.
  - NHN KCP (and 나이스정보통신, grouped with it on the page): about 3.2%, with KCP's annual fee waived.
- Small-merchant tiers: annual revenue up to 3억원 counts as 영세, and 3억 to 30억원 as 중소. Lower tiers get lower rates, and PGs update the tier quarterly. Snippet. — [토스페이먼츠 블로그](https://www.tosspayments.com/blog/articles/semo-135)
- New businesses start at the general rate. Once revenue is reported (about 6 months), qualifying 영세 or 중소 merchants get a one-time partial refund. Snippet. — [토스페이먼츠 블로그](https://www.tosspayments.com/blog/articles/54491?from=category)

### Inferences
- Checkout should offer Naver Pay, KakaoPay and Toss Pay. Naver Pay leads overall, KakaoPay matches the KakaoTalk sharing flow, and Toss is strong with teens.
- Example fee at a 4,900원 price and 3.4%: about 167원 plus VAT, about 184원 per sale before fixed fees. At 9,900원 it is about 370원 including VAT. Fixed costs (about 220,000원 sign-up, about 110,000원 a year at Toss Payments) need roughly 70 to 80 sales of 4,900원 to cover in the first year. This is simple arithmetic from the cited rates, not a quote.
- Avoid mobile-phone billing (6 to 9%, D+30) as a main method for small tickets. It is popular with teens but expensive.

### Gaps
- No 2025 full-year Bank of Korea split between electronic financial companies and phone makers was retrieved (only the H1 figure, 55.1% vs 23.9%).
- Per-PG rates beyond Toss Payments and KCP were in an image table on the PortOne page and could not be read.
- No current published fee schedule from Naver Pay, KakaoPay or Toss Pay for small direct merchants was found.
